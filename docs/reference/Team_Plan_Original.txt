1. Project Overview
The goal of this project is to design, implement, and evaluate a multimodal biometric authentication system that combines facial recognition and palmprint recognition. By using two distinct biometric characteristics, we aim to investigate how combining authentication methods affects system accuracy, reliability, and performance under different image-quality conditions.
Our system will be developed in Python using publicly available, professor-approved biometric datasets. We will implement separate authentication pipelines for facial and palmprint images, including image preprocessing, feature extraction, and similarity-based matching. Both systems will generate genuine and impostor matching scores, which will be evaluated using biometric performance metrics such as Equal Error Rate (EER), d-prime, and score distribution analysis.
The project will investigate three connected research questions through a series of experiments. First, we will establish baseline performance by comparing face-only and palmprint-only authentication using clear, good-quality images. Next, we will implement equal-weight score-level fusion, combining both modalities' normalized matching scores to determine how fusion affects authentication accuracy compared with either modality individually.
Finally, we will introduce progressively stronger Gaussian blur to facial authentication images while maintaining consistent palmprint image quality. This experiment will simulate situations involving camera movement, poor focus, or subject motion, allowing us to investigate whether palmprint authentication helps maintain performance when facial image quality deteriorates.
Throughout development, we will maintain consistent experimental conditions, document our implementation decisions, and analyze how each experiment informs the next. Our final deliverables will include a functional Python authentication system, quantitative experimental results, a concise technical report discussing our methodology and findings, and a group presentation communicating our overall results and conclusions.

2. Research Questions & Experimental Design
Our project investigates how combining facial recognition and palmprint recognition affects biometric authentication performance, particularly when facial image quality deteriorates.We will conduct three connected experiments, beginning with individual biometric authentication, followed by score-level fusion, and finally evaluating the combined system under increasing facial image blur.
RQ1: Baseline Authentication Performance
Research Question: Under clear, good-quality image conditions, how do face-only and palmprint-only authentication systems compare in terms of Equal Error Rate (EER)?
Objective: Establish a baseline for both biometric modalities before introducing fusion or image degradation.
Implementation:
Select face and palmprint datasets containing usable, good-quality images.
Standardize image dimensions and preprocessing for each modality.
Develop separate face and palmprint authentication pipelines.
Generate genuine and impostor matching scores for each system.
Calculate and compare EER to determine their individual baseline performance.
Expected Outcome: Two baseline EER values that establish how each modality performs independently. These results will guide our evaluation of score-level fusion in RQ2.
RQ2: Equal-Weight Score-Level Fusion
Research Question: How does equal-weight fusion of face and palmprint matching scores affect EER compared with using either biometric modality individually under the same baseline conditions?
Objective: Determine whether combining both modalities improves, reduces, or has no meaningful effect on authentication performance.
Implementation:
Use the matching scores generated by the face and palmprint systems in RQ1.
Normalize both sets of scores to a comparable scale.
Combine the corresponding scores using an equal-weight average (50% face, 50% palmprint).
Calculate the fused system's EER and compare it with the individual baseline EER values.
Analyze whether fusion provides an advantage over either modality alone.
Expected Outcome: A direct comparison of face-only, palmprint-only, and fused authentication performance. The findings will establish how the combined system performs before introducing facial image blur in RQ3.
RQ3: Authentication Performance Under Facial Image Blur
Research Question: As facial image blur increases while palmprint image quality remains unchanged, how does equal-weight fusion affect EER compared with face-only and palmprint-only authentication?
Objective: Evaluate whether multimodal authentication can maintain reliable performance when facial image quality decreases.
Implementation:
Begin with the baseline face and palmprint datasets established in RQ1.
Apply progressively stronger Gaussian blur to facial images while keeping palmprint images unchanged.
Recalculate facial matching scores at each blur level using the existing authentication pipeline.
Combine the degraded facial scores with the unchanged palmprint scores using the fusion method from RQ2.
Measure and compare EER across face-only, palmprint-only, and fused systems at each blur level.
Real-World Relevance: Facial images may become blurry due to camera movement, poor focus, or subject motion during authentication. Meanwhile, a palmprint captured separately under more controlled conditions may remain clear. This experiment simulates that situation to investigate whether an unaffected biometric modality can help maintain authentication performance.
Expected Outcome: An EER comparison across increasing facial blur levels, illustrating how image degradation affects individual and combined authentication performance.
